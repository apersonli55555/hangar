## Thesis

Lockheed Martin's remaining performance obligations (RPO) rose from $179.1 billion at
September 28, 2025
([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/936468/000162828025045693/0001628280-25-045693-index.htm))
to $292.0 billion at September 27, 2026
([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/936468/000162828026066928/0001628280-26-066928-index.htm)),
up 63%. Most of the increase came in two quarters: RPO was $230.4 billion at June 28, 2026
([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/936468/000162828026049411/0001628280-26-049411-index.htm)).

Revenue grew from $71.0 billion in 2024
([10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/936468/000093646825000009/0000936468-25-000009-index.htm))
to $75.0 billion in 2025
([10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/936468/000162828026004195/0001628280-26-004195-index.htm)),
about 6%. RPO is now about 3.9 times 2025 revenue.

My view: backlog growing ten times faster than revenue should lift revenue growth above 6%.

## Evidence

- From April to September 2026, Hangar counts $32.4 billion of definite awards to Lockheed
  Martin in Department of War announcements.
- Two more single awards in that period, $35.3 billion on June 24
  ([announcement](https://web.archive.org/web/20260714155829/https://www.war.gov/News/Contracts/Contract/Article/4525817/contracts-for-june-24-2026/))
  and $53.9 billion on July 30
  ([announcement](https://web.archive.org/web/20260805110841/https://www.war.gov/News/Contracts/Contract/Article/4560267/contracts-for-july-30-2026/)),
  are held out of Hangar's totals until a person confirms them against the source, as every
  amount over $10 billion is.

## Catalysts

- The 2026 10-K and the 2027 quarterly reports: does revenue growth move above 6%?

## Risks / what would prove me wrong

- Multiyear contracts are delivered over many years, so a large backlog can turn into revenue slowly.
- Fixed-price programs can lose money if costs run over.
- I'm wrong if revenue for the four quarters ending September 2027 grows 6% or less compared
  with the four quarters before.

## Update 2026-11-09

Adding the price side of the pitch, which the original left out.

**12-month price target: $630 (up 11%).** If revenue over the next four quarters grows 10% to
about $87,030 million and Lockheed keeps its 1.8x EV/sales, enterprise value reaches about
$160,090 million; subtracting $20,498 million debt, adding $5,960 million cash and dividing by
231.0 million shares gives $630.08. I keep the multiple flat, so the target comes only from
revenue growth.

**What changes the call:** I'm wrong on the stock if it is below $567.08 in November 2027, or if it
trails SPY's return over the same 12 months.

## Update 2026-11-10

Adding a second valuation check (EV/EBITDA) and Lockheed's own guidance.

**How EV/EBITDA is calculated here.** EBITDA is operating income plus depreciation and amortization, both over the last four reported quarters, taken from each company's SEC filings (the full fiscal year, plus this year so far, minus the same months a year earlier). It is my calculation, not a figure the companies report. Enterprise value is the same one used in the EV/sales comparison above, at the November 6 closing prices.

- **LMT: 13.2x.** EBITDA $11,002 million: operating income $9,316 million plus D&A $1,686 million, over the four quarters to September 27, 2026 ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/936468/000162828026066928/0001628280-26-066928-index.htm), [10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/936468/000162828026004195/0001628280-26-004195-index.htm)).
- **NOC: 14.0x.** EBITDA $6,149 million: operating income $4,625 million plus D&A $1,524 million, to September 30, 2026 ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1133421/000113342126000038/0001133421-26-000038-index.htm), [10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1133421/000113342126000003/0001133421-26-000003-index.htm)).
- **General Dynamics (GD): 14.4x.** EBITDA $6,809 million: operating income $5,859 million plus D&A $950 million, to October 4, 2026 ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/40533/000004053326000038/0000040533-26-000038-index.htm), [10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/40533/000004053326000006/0000040533-26-000006-index.htm)).
- **RTX: 18.1x.** EBITDA $15,676 million: operating income $11,299 million plus D&A $4,377 million, to September 30, 2026 ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/101829/000010182926000031/0000101829-26-000031-index.htm), [10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/101829/000010182926000006/0000101829-26-000006-index.htm)).

Lockheed is the cheapest of the four on EBITDA too, so both checks agree. As a cross-check on the target: at Northrop's 14.0x, Lockheed's current EBITDA alone would give an enterprise value of about $154,030 million, or about **$604 a share** after debt and cash (my calculation).

**Guidance.** In its [third-quarter 2026 results](https://news.lockheedmartin.com/2026-10-22-Lockheed-Martin-Reports-Third-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results) on October 22 ([8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/936468/000162828026066780/0001628280-26-066780-index.htm)), Lockheed raised its 2026 outlook: sales of about $80,000 to $82,000 million (was $79,750 to $81,750 million), earnings per share of $30.35 to $30.75 and free cash flow of $7,800 to $8,000 million. The middle of the sales range, $81,000 million, is 7.9% above 2025's $75,048 million ([10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/936468/000162828026004195/0001628280-26-004195-index.htm)).

**What this means for the target.** My $630 target assumed revenue grows 10% over the next four quarters. Lockheed's own guidance implies about 8% for 2026. So the target needs growth to speed up in 2027, which is what the backlog suggests but the company has not yet guided to. I'm keeping $630, and I now see more risk in the growth assumption than in the multiple.
