## Thesis

GE Aerospace's remaining performance obligations (RPO) jumped from $190.6 billion at
December 31, 2025
([10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/40545/000004054526000008/0000040545-26-000008-index.htm))
to $211.3 billion at March 31, 2026
([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/40545/000004054526000027/0000040545-26-000027-index.htm)),
then stopped: $210.8 billion at June 30
([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/40545/000004054526000049/0000040545-26-000049-index.htm))
and $212.1 billion at September 30, 2026
([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/40545/000004054526000064/0000040545-26-000064-index.htm)).

Revenue grew from $38.7 billion in 2024
([10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/40545/000004054525000015/0000040545-25-000015-index.htm))
to $45.9 billion in 2025 (10-K above), about 18%.

My view: when backlog stops growing, revenue growth that fast is hard to keep up, so I
expect it to slow.

## Evidence

- Two flat quarters of RPO, from the 10-Qs above. That is the whole case so far, and it is thin:
  one data series over six months.
- GE's earlier history is hard to compare because of the GE HealthCare (2023) and GE Vernova
  (2024) spin-offs, so I only use figures from 2025 on.

## Catalysts

- The 2026 10-K and the 2027 quarterly reports: RPO and revenue growth.

## Risks / what would prove me wrong

- Two flat quarters may just be timing: large orders arrive unevenly.
- Most of GE's revenue is commercial aircraft engines and services, which this site's defense
  data barely covers.
- I'm wrong if RPO is above $230 billion by September 2027, or if revenue for the four quarters
  ending September 2027 grows 15% or more compared with the four quarters before.
