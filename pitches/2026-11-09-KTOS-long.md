## Thesis

Kratos' remaining performance obligations (RPO) rose from $1.41 billion at June 29, 2025
([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1069258/000106925825000048/0001069258-25-000048-index.htm))
to $2.65 billion at September 27, 2026
([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1069258/000106925826000088/0001069258-26-000088-index.htm)),
up 87%. Revenue grew from $1.14 billion in 2024
([10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1069258/000106925825000008/0001069258-25-000008-index.htm))
to $1.35 billion in 2025
([10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1069258/000106925826000013/0001069258-26-000013-index.htm)),
up 19%. RPO is now about twice 2025 revenue.

My view: revenue growth stays near or above 2025's 19% over the next year, because the
backlog grew far faster than revenue.

## Evidence

- Since November 2025, Hangar has matched only three Department of War announcements
  to Kratos, and none of them has a definite amount: two indefinite-delivery contracts and one
  shared pool. So the backlog growth came from work that is not in the daily announcements of
  $7.5 million or more: smaller awards, subcontracts, other agencies or other customers.
- That also means contract announcements are a poor way to follow Kratos; its 10-Q is the
  better source.

## Catalysts

- The 2026 10-K and the 2027 quarterly reports: revenue growth and RPO.

## Risks / what would prove me wrong

- I don't yet know which contracts make up the new backlog; if most of it is long-dated,
  revenue will lag.
- Kratos is small, so a single program delay moves its numbers.
- I'm wrong if revenue for the four quarters ending September 2027 grows less than 15%
  compared with the four quarters before, or if RPO falls below $2.0 billion.

## Valuation

No view on valuation here: this is a call on revenue growth, and Hangar shows no prices.
