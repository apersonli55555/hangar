Methodology

Hangar turns the Department of War's daily contract announcements into a table of awards per company. This page explains where the numbers come from, what is and isn't counted, and what the data cannot tell you.

Source

Each business day around 5 p.m. Eastern, the Department of War publishes every contract worth $7.5 million or more at war.gov/News/Contracts, as plain paragraphs of text. War.gov blocks automated readers, and Hangar does not try to get around that. Instead it reads the copies saved by the Internet Archive, which usually appear within a day. Every award on this site links to both the official page and the archived copy it was read from.

Contract value is not revenue. A contract is paid out over months or years, options may never be exercised, and IDIQ ceilings are upper limits. Use these numbers to see what was won and when, not to estimate a quarter's sales.

How the text is read

A program reads each paragraph by explicit rules, never by guessing: the company is the subject of "was / is being / has been awarded"; the amount is the first figure after it, or a figure in a fixed later phrasing such as "The total value of this contract is $X". When a rule doesn't apply cleanly, the field is left empty and the record is marked needs review instead of being filled in. Every rule is tested against real paragraphs from the source.{% if repo_url %} The code and full dataset are open source.{% endif %}

How companies are matched to stocks

Announcements name the legal entity that signed the contract, often a subsidiary ("Sikorsky Aircraft Corp.", "Electric Boat"). A list of name rules maps these to the parent's ticker, with dates where ownership changed (for example, Aerojet Rocketdyne counts as L3Harris only from July 28, 2023). Joint ventures are never assigned to a ticker, because their value is split between owners in ways the announcement doesn't state. Rules marked "(verify)" come from general knowledge and are being checked against each parent's subsidiary list in its SEC 10-K filing.

Backlog, revenue and materiality

A $500 million award is huge for Kratos and a rounding error for Lockheed Martin. To show that, each company page charts two figures from the company's own SEC filings, and each definite award is shown as a percentage of them.

  • Source: SEC EDGAR's free XBRL company facts: the numbers companies tag in their 10-K and 10-Q filings. Every value links to the filing it came from.
  • Backlog = remaining performance obligations (RPO), tag RevenueRemainingPerformanceObligation: contracted work not yet delivered. It is usually smaller than the "backlog" in press releases, which often adds unfunded orders and options. No standard tag exists for that larger figure, and Hangar doesn't read it from 10-K text.
  • Revenue: one tag per company (Revenues or RevenueFromContractWithCustomerExcludingAssessedTax), whichever covers the most recent fiscal years, so a series never mixes definitions. The choice is listed below.
  • Fiscal years follow each company's own calendar (TransDigm's ends in September). A period is a year if it is about 365 days long and a quarter if about 91; 6- and 9-month year-to-date figures are not used.
  • As first reported. Each 10-K repeats last year's numbers. Hangar keeps the first filing that reported a period, because that is what investors knew at the time. If a later filing changed the number, the revision is shown next to it with its own link.
  • Award ÷ RPO uses the latest RPO whose filing was public on the announcement date, and award ÷ revenue the latest full fiscal year whose 10-K was public on that date. "Public" means filed, not period-ended: a quarter ending June 30 isn't known until the 10-Q comes out weeks later. Using later data would be look-ahead.
  • Only definite awards get a percentage. Ceilings and IDIQ maximums are not money, so a percentage would overstate them.
  • Gaps are shown, not filled. If a company doesn't tag a figure, or skips a period, the page says so. Nothing is estimated.

What this data can't tell you

  • Subcontracts are invisible. Component makers (TransDigm, Howmet and others) mostly sell through prime contractors, so they rarely appear here even when their defense business is large.
  • Awards under $7.5 million aren't announced.
  • Classified awards are not published.
  • Timing: announcements come after the market closes; a later, structured record of the same contracts appears on USAspending.gov about 90 days later.

Why there are no stock prices here

The free stock-price services we checked (Tiingo, Massive/Polygon, Alpha Vantage) license their free data for personal use only and do not allow showing it, or returns calculated from it, on a public website. Hangar follows those terms, so it shows no prices and no price reactions. Each award instead links to that stock's daily prices around the announcement date on Yahoo Finance, so you can look for yourself. Announcements are posted after the market closes, so the first trading day that can react is the next one.

Not investment advice

Hangar is a student research project. It reports what the government announced; it does not recommend buying or selling any security. Check the source before relying on any number.
